# The Naming: Synarchy Framework

## The Etymology
The name **Synarchy** is derived from the Greek roots *syn-* (together) and *arkhia* (rule/government). While "monarchy" is the rule of one, and "hierarchy" is the rule of rank, **Synarchy** represents a system of joint sovereignty or shared governance.

## Why this name?
In the era of AI-driven software engineering, the traditional terminology of "AI-Assisted Coding" fails to capture the complexity of modern delivery. We believe that building software today requires a new social contract between human intelligence and artificial agency.

1. **Software Engineering, not just Scripting:** We are not just generating snippets; we are building complex systems. This requires a "government" — a set of strict rules, predefined roles, and normative flows.
2. **The 20% Sovereignty:** While AI agents can perform the "heavy lifting" (80%) of code generation across any stack, the human architect holds the ultimate responsibility for the 20% that defines security, business logic, and architectural integrity.
3. **Structured Collaboration:** **Synarchy** was chosen because it perfectly describes this state of **Joint Governance**. It’s not about building AI agents; it’s about the methodology of building **software** with them.

## Core Pillars
*   **The Architect (The Human):** Holds the veto power, defines the "Aegis" (protection), and governs the final 20% of the implementation.
*   **The Agents (The AI):** Operate within a controlled and normative context (e.g., a dedicated `.agents/` directory or specialized environment), following strict rules to deliver the 80% of the workload.
*   **The Protocol (The Framework):** The set of base files and flows that ensure the software remains architecturally sound, regardless of which entity touched the code last.

## Pronunciation
The intended international pronunciation is:
> **SY-nar-chy** `/ˈsɪn.ɑːr.ki/`

The "ch" is pronounced with a hard **"k"** sound (as in *Architecture* or *Monarchy*), reinforcing the technical and structural nature of the framework.
